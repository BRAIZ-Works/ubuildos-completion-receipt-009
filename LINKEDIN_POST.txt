Day 8 of building in public: a customer-ops team gets a mixed request queue.

Some are billing questions.
Some are support issues.
Some are sales requests.
Some are not clear enough to route safely.

The problem isn't receiving the request.
It's knowing who owns it and what should happen next without guessing.

So I built Customer Request Router™ around one simple question:
“What should happen next when a customer request arrives?”

Known request types follow an explicit routing table.
Every item shows type, urgency, owner, and next action.
Missing, unsupported, or ambiguous inputs become REVIEW with the reason still visible.
The current visible queue can be exported as JSON or CSV.

No account.
No CRM integration.
No autonomous external actions.
No real customer data.

The app is intentionally small. The test is bigger than the app.
A capable developer could recreate parts of this quickly. That isn't the claim.

UBuildOS is being tested on whether it can repeatedly take a bounded problem from intake to a working, inspectable, independently verified public release - while preserving the requirements, evidence, recovery path, live readback, and exact completion record.

Simple builds make that test cleaner. The screen may be simple. The test is the whole path around it.

What Day 08 proves
• Supported request types route deterministically.
• Unclear inputs fail closed to visible REVIEW instead of an invented route.
• The routing decision remains inspectable.
• The visible state is exportable.

What it does NOT prove
• Live CRM or ticketing integrations.
• Autonomous actions.
• Production-scale readiness.
• Business impact, market demand, or superiority.

The product review reinforced one rule: uncertainty needs a first-class state. Hiding it would make the queue look cleaner and the decision less trustworthy.

Inspect the product. Challenge the routing pattern.
